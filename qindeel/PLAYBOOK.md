# قِنديل · Qindeel — account playbook

Working name: **قِنديل** ("lantern"). One idea carries the whole account: a traveler walks a
night road with a lantern, and every good lesson makes the light grow until dawn breaks.
The name, the visual motif and the lesson are the same thing, which is what makes it recognisable
after two or three videos. The name is a proposal — change `brand` in the episode file and it
updates everywhere.

## 2. The format (every video)

| Second | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Hook**: a surprising outcome, with the cause hidden ("a deed that takes a moment… Allah forgave a man for it") | Viewers decide in the first 2 seconds |
| 3–12 | **Story**: the traveler meets one thing on the road. One action, no more | Movement holds attention |
| 12–18 | **Proof**: the hadith or verse, exact wording, with its source and number | Trust; people share what they can verify |
| 18–23 | **Lesson** in one line, plus a supporting verse | The takeaway they repeat |
| 23–28 | **Question** to the comments + follow line | Comments and shares drive reach |

Rules that stay fixed: 20–30 seconds; Arabic on top, English subtitle below on every line;
no musical instruments (ambient sound only, safe for the whole audience); no faces drawn;
never depict prophets or companions; every text carries its source.

## 4. Posting rhythm

- **Weeks 1–4:** 1 reel a day at **9:00 pm Makkah time** (peak for Gulf, Egypt and the Levant;
  afternoon in North Africa). Daily posting matters most while the account has no history.
- **After week 4:** 5 reels a week, plus 2 carousels (the hadith as swipeable cards — these get saved).
- **Fridays:** a Jumu'ah-themed episode (Surah al-Kahf, salawat, the hour of response).
- **Stories daily:** a question box or poll tied to that day's reel.

## 6. Growing the audience

1. **Shares are the metric.** Islamic content spreads through "send this to someone" far more
   than through likes. Every caption ends with a share prompt, every video with a question.
2. **Reply to every comment in the first hour**, with a question back where it fits.
3. **Pin one comment** on each reel: the full hadith text and source.
4. **Series naming.** Title the episodes ("قنديل ١", "قنديل ٢"…) so viewers look for the others.
5. **Cross-post the same file** to TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels. Same work, four chances.
6. **Collaborate** with 5–10 small Arabic Islamic pages (5k–50k followers) using Instagram's
   Collab post, once there are 10+ reels up.
7. **Seasonal series** planned ahead: Ramadan (30 episodes), Dhul-Hijjah (10), Fridays.
8. **Weekly review** (send me the insights screenshot): keep the hooks with the best 3-second
   hold rate and shares per view, drop the worst, and make two variations of the winner.

Realistic expectation: a new account with daily, consistent reels usually needs 3–6 weeks before
one video breaks out. The first month is about finding which hooks work, not follower count.

## 7. Before publishing

- Have someone with Islamic knowledge you trust review each script. I check wording and
  references against published sources, but I am not a scholar and the account carries your name.
- Sound: the pilot uses ambient sound only (wind, footsteps, one chime, birds). A voice-over in
  Arabic would raise watch time noticeably if you want to record one or have one generated.
